## Ethics of Deep Learning Models

I believe that deep learning models, such as DALL-E and Chat GPT generating creative content can cause multiple ethical issues (Bergmann, 2025). The question brought up image and prose generation, both of which have been contentious in this age of AI generated content. For starters, AI generated content cannot be copyrighted according to US copyright laws (Zirpoli, 2025). AI generated content is being generated using the banks of millions or billions of entries of human creativity and knowledge, more often than not without their consent (Zirpoli, 2025). Some may argue that this is theft and AI should be paying and credited the human artists and workers it is generating its prompts from. Because AI generation technology is so new, I think it will take governments and laws decades for it to catch up with AI and the credits it should give to human authors. I believe the reputation of AI generated creative works is very negative, and the more the technology advances, the harder it is to tell that it is AI content. From what I have seen, it appears that corporations and small businesses like to use AI to speed up their creative processes (Bolies, 2026). The movie and television industry is a good example of a creative field embracing the use of generative AI, at the detriment and protest of its workers and consumers. Artists and the general population do not have a good impression about AI generated content, because they are aware it was not made by a human. AI generated content feels like deception or theft to people, so I would not say it is ethically right to be using it.
